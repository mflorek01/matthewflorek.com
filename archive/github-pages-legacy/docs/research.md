# Personal Site Research

Date: March 12, 2026

## Scope

This research focuses on strong personal websites for professionals working across data, analytics, AI, and technical communication. Matthew's resume points toward business intelligence, data operations, analytics engineering, and AI-assisted workflow work, so the examples below were selected for that lane rather than for designers or software-only portfolios.

## Example Sites Reviewed

### 1. Chip Huyen

Site: https://huyenchip.com/

What works:
- The homepage establishes credibility immediately with a clear identity, book links, research, and practical AI work.
- Navigation is narrow and intentional: writing, talks, projects, and bio are easy to find.
- The design feels serious without being corporate; typography and spacing do most of the work.

What to borrow:
- Lead with a concise professional position before asking the visitor to dig deeper.
- Make the strongest proof items visible in the first screen instead of hiding them behind a menu.

### 2. Eugene Yan

Site: https://eugeneyan.com/

What works:
- The homepage is built around useful proof: writing, speaking, consulting, and featured work.
- The site feels current because it surfaces recent output rather than a generic biography.
- The information architecture is simple enough that a recruiter can scan it in under a minute.

What to borrow:
- Give visitors one fast path to understand expertise and one fast path to contact.
- Show a small amount of selected proof rather than a long undifferentiated list.

### 3. Cassie Kozyrkov

Site: https://kozyrkov.com/

What works:
- The site has a strong point of view and a memorable visual identity.
- Social proof is obvious early through books, speaking, and recognizable organizations.
- The homepage balances authority and accessibility well.

What to borrow:
- Treat the site like a professional front door, not just a document dump.
- Use one distinct visual language so the page feels authored rather than templated.

### 4. Hamel Husain

Site: https://hamel.dev/

What works:
- The homepage is dense with proof, but it is still readable because the information is grouped well.
- The site makes projects, writing, notebooks, and community activity easy to inspect.
- It signals technical depth quickly without relying on a long abstract summary.

What to borrow:
- Technical professionals benefit from showing concrete outputs.
- As Matthew adds case studies later, they should be short, specific, and linkable.

### 5. Christina Kaye

Site: https://www.christinakaye.com/

What works:
- The homepage is easy for hiring managers to scan because it puts personal introduction, work samples, and resume access near the top.
- It feels approachable and professional without trying to imitate a startup landing page.
- The structure is useful for an early-career data professional because it does not depend on a huge archive of writing or talks.

What to borrow:
- For a first version, clarity matters more than volume.
- A compact site with a headshot, resume, and one or two clear skill groupings can still feel complete.

## Patterns Shared By The Best Sites

- They state who the person is and what kind of work they do in one glance.
- They remove friction around contact, resume access, and credibility signals.
- They show proof fast, but they do not flood the visitor with every past activity.
- They use a consistent visual point of view instead of a generic portfolio template.
- They are easy to scan on mobile and on a laptop in a recruiting workflow.

## What That Means For Matthew's First Version

Matthew does not need a bloated portfolio yet. Based on the examples above, the best first release is:

- A strong hero with name, professional focus, contact actions, and a portrait area.
- A short capability snapshot organized around analytics, automation, and AI-assisted work.
- An embedded resume with a download link.
- A simple contact section with email, phone, and LinkedIn.

That approach matches what strong professional sites do well:

- clear positioning
- immediate credibility
- low-friction contact
- room to grow later

## Recommended Expansion After V1

- Add two or three short case studies once Matthew has work samples he can publish.
- Add a real headshot to replace the temporary visual placeholder.
- Add a custom domain and analytics only after the content is stable.
- Add writing or notes if he wants to signal thoughtfulness and current learning.
